Salmon Simulator

A training simulator for live sound in Chapman's Salmon Recital Hall, built on real photos of the room. The goal is to teach you to see the system: notice a problem, trace the signal, fix it, and prove the fix works.

Explore

Walk around Salmon

Walk the hall photo by photo, from the entrance to FOH, the stage racks and the storage closet. Tap equipment to zoom in, or use "Walk to…" to get somewhere. The editing tools work too, but nothing is saved; Export JSON downloads your changes as a file.

It's 4:50 and rehearsal starts at 5:00. Pick your job: the mics (batteries into 16 wireless mics, each checked at the X32) or the monitors (carry the wedges out, cable and power them, prove them with the Wii Shop theme). Then the line check: every singer, every wedge. Things go wrong: find out why, fix it, and prove it works.

Where it's at

NOTICE→TRACE→ACT→VERIFY
  1. The room, walkableReal photos linked into a map of the hall, with turns, walking routes and zoom-in views of the gear.
    __SCENES__ scenes__LINKS__ links__INSETS__ equipment close-ups
  2. The first gameplay loopHandheld → PTU-6000 receiver → X32 input. Batteries from the charger, a mic that won't switch on, a "bad mic" that has to be set aside for a spare, a hint ladder, and a debrief. A mic only counts when you've seen its signal at the X32: fixed isn't the same as verified.
  3. The monitorsWedges carried from storage (a strength check to lift them), one speakON run from the NX3000's Channel A daisy-chained to three wedges, the Furman then the amp, and the Wii Shop theme through Bus 1 to prove it. A cable in the wrong channel, or a speakON that didn't lock.
  4. The line checkEach singer says something while the A1 watches their meter, then checks they hear themselves in the wedges. Switched-off and muted mics, quiet singers, feedback, a dead monitor amp, and the A2 sent out to help.
  5. Next: the game on the photos themselvesFewer boxes and buttons: pick up mics, the charger and the wedges right in the pictures.

People in the photos are replaced with generic figures. Hardware, layout and procedures follow the real room as far as they are known; unknown details are marked as placeholders in the project, not invented.
